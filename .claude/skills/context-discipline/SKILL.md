# Context Discipline — The Full Design

The invariants live in `CLAUDE.md` → "Context Discipline" and govern at all times. This skill carries the reasoning behind each, the mechanisms that implement them, and the broken forms that were tried or proposed — read it in full before changing any of them.

The harness replays a session's **entire** persisted transcript to the model on every wake. Three consequences follow, and all three are invariants — a change that violates any of them is a defect no test will fail on and no log will name, because the agent keeps working, just at a silently rising cost. (Root cause this guards against: issue #275 — @glm-5.2 reached **754,201 input tokens per call** in three days, 47% of it ~66 stale copies of the ephemeral per-wake brief, 39% raw tool output; and issue #276, the wall that growth was heading for.)

- **Nothing replayed per wake may be unbounded.** This is the invariant the other two serve, and it is the one to reason from: *every* class of content in the message list must have something that bounds it, or the agent eventually walks into its model's context ceiling — where the provider 400s deterministically and every later wake rebuilds the same over-long request and fails identically, bricking the agent on that timeline until a human edits its session file. Today the bounds are: the brief (never persisted), tool results (capped at `TOOL_RESULT_CAP`), **a tool call's arguments** (capped at `TOOL_ARGS_CAP` — #301), **both of them per *step*, not per call** (#304), images (evicted after the turn that showed them), and **the conversation itself** (compacted past half the model's context ceiling — `_context.py`, triggered on the provider's *own* reported `tokens_in`, never a client-side count), whose **summary note** is bounded in turn: it must be strictly smaller than the region it replaces or the compaction declines, and its code-harvested identifier block is capped at `IDENTIFIER_CAP` (issue #561). Adding a new *kind* of content to the transcript without deciding what bounds it is the defect; "it's small" is not a bound, because everything in a standing agent's transcript is paid for on every wake, forever.
- **Stable content first, volatile content last — the cache invariant.** Provider prefix caching only pays out on a byte-stable prefix, so the frozen transcript goes *first* and anything recomposed per wake (the operating brief: current time, step budget, live dashboard) is spliced in at the **tail**, immediately before the newest user turn. The instinctive refactor — "system prompts go first," moving the brief to position 0 — changes the prefix on every request and **silently destroys caching fleet-wide** (measured live: a `cached_tokens: 238277` hit billed at the cache-read rate, ~5.4× cheaper input). It costs nothing to break and nothing visible fails when you do. Do not reorder the context without measuring `cached_tokens`.
- **Only the conversation persists; everything else is ephemeral or capped.** A thing the model *reads* is not automatically a thing the transcript *keeps*. Ephemeral-by-construction context (the brief — a snapshot of a moment) is shown per wake and never written; a persisted copy is a stale duplicate the agent re-reads and re-pays for forever. Raw tool output is shown in full on the turn it ran and **capped on persistence** (head + tail around an elision marker naming the original size — `_session.py`, `TOOL_RESULT_CAP`, 4 KB), so one mailbox listing or wide file read is not a permanent tax on the life of the timeline. The precedent is the engine's image eviction (a viewed image is seen once, never re-billed); text got the same discipline in #275. When adding anything to the message list, decide *deliberately* which of the three it is — conversation (persists), ephemeral (never persists), or bulk output (persists capped). **What the model reads and what the disk keeps are now separated by construction** (#297): a save can land *mid-turn*, when the model is still looking at the image and still reading the full result it got back — so bounding happens **on the way out** (`_session._payload`, on dicts), never by mutating the live list. A refactor that "simplifies" that by capping `history` in place reaches into the conversation the engine is holding and takes things out of it.

Two things follow from the compaction half that are easy to break by accident, so they are stated here rather than left in the code: **the two persistence caps are load-bearing prerequisites of the compaction threshold, not neighbors of it** — compaction fires *between* turns, so it is only safe to sit at 50% of the ceiling because one turn's persisted growth is bounded (`TOOL_RESULT_CAP` + `TOOL_ARGS_CAP` = 6 KB per **step** × 24 steps ≈ 49 K tokens — per *step*, whatever its fan-out; see below) and therefore cannot leap the gap; raise either cap or the step budget far enough and the threshold stops being a safe distance. And **a compaction cut may land only immediately before a *real* `user` turn** — a cut mid-tool-chain strands a `tool` result whose assistant call was dropped, and a dangling `tool_call_id` is malformed *permanently*, breaking every later wake. When no safe cut exists the compactor declines and says so; that is the correct failure, and a "fix" that makes it cut anyway is the worst bug this design guards against. *"Real"* is doing work in that sentence (#297): the engine and the code bridge both inject `user`-role turns that are a turn's own **work**, not a new turn of the conversation, and an injected turn treated as a boundary can become the "newest user turn always survives" **floor** — at which point compaction keeps the image caption and summarizes the peer's actual message away. Valid transcript, broken agent: the delivery guarantee's recovery can no longer find the turn that carried a message. `Message.injected` marks them; `_cut_index` skips them.

**Measure a bound in the characters the model reads, never in the bytes the disk escapes** (#301 review, and it shipped in the first draft of that fix). `json.dumps` defaults to `ensure_ascii=True`, which expands every non-Latin character into a six-character `\uXXXX`. Size a cap with it and the cap silently bites **six times harder on every non-Latin script**: an ordinary 500-character Japanese message body "measured" 3,000 and blew a 2,048 cap, so a peer answered in Japanese kept nothing but its `action` — its own words, its timeline uuid, its subject, gone from its memory — while the identical message in English persisted whole. Nothing errors, no test fails, and the agent is simply worse at being an agent in that language. A cap here bounds **context**, and context is billed in tokens computed from the *decoded* string; what the file costs on disk is a different question nobody is asking. The same applies to any future accounting of what a wake replays (`_session._json_size`, `_context.message_chars`). *On a platform whose founding claim is that its peers are equals, an agent that cannot remember what it said because of the language it said it in is not a cost bug.*

**A cap must degrade, never collapse.** The first draft cut the biggest argument until the call fit — and an excerpt costs a marker, so an argument can be *too small to be worth eliding and still too big to keep*. A call made of several such arguments (an ordinary `tasks create` with three 700-character fields) could not be brought under the cap at all, and fell through to the total-loss stub: **every** argument dropped to save the 159 characters that were over. The fix is water-filling (`_session._fit`) — hand every argument an equal share of the budget, so a short one is kept byte for byte and rolls its surplus over to the long one. It always makes progress, and it takes the room from where the room actually is. Any bound added here needs the same property: the failure mode of a cap should be a smaller excerpt, never a shrug. (And the fit is **measured, not computed** — escaping is content-dependent, so each excerpt is cut by what it costs *serialized* (`_session._within`), and the loop that checks the real size and halves is only the backstop; an arithmetic slip then costs a shorter excerpt, never the bound. Cutting by character count against a serialized budget was that slip, and it was not small: every line break in a message overshot the fit, so the halving fired on nearly every real long message and threw away half its budget.)

**A tool call's `arguments` are bounded too, and the *exception* is the whole design** (#301). They were the last unbounded class — `_payload` capped a `tool` result's content and wrote the `tool_calls` whole, so an `assets create` carrying a 200 KB document re-sent that document to the model on every turn for the life of the timeline. What made it hard is that the naive cap is *worse than the bug*: the resume replays an interrupted platform create **from exactly those arguments**, so eliding them re-posts the peer's message with its body cut out — and if the dead wake's POST never landed, the elided body is what the timeline keeps. So the cap keys on **replayability, not size**: an *interrupted platform create* (its healed "outcome unknown" result is the flag) keeps its arguments whole, and everything else — every settled call, and every interrupted call the recovery will *never* re-run, because no key can un-spend money at fal.ai — is bounded from the first save. The exception is therefore bounded in **count** (one dead turn's creates) and in **duration** (the re-issue writes a real result over the marker, and the next save caps them). Two corollaries that a "cleanup" will otherwise undo: the cap is applied **on the way out** (`_payload`, on dicts) and *never* in place, because a save lands mid-turn and an in-place cap would reach into both the call the engine is dispatching and the body a resumed wake is about to re-post; and capping may **never change what `create_kind` reads** off a call, or a create vanishes from the recovery's count, the ordinal drifts, and a key the platform has never seen is a message posted twice.

**An elision marker is text the model reads, so it is text the model can write** (issue #576). Every wake replays the capped transcript, so the model reads its own past speech as head, marker, tail. @briggs, writing a long reply, reproduced that shape, marker and all, and the harness posted it: a fragment on the platform whose own words said *"the full value was sent when the call ran"*. The cap was the first suspect and was not at fault. The live dispatch runs the calls the adapter just parsed; the cap runs on a copy for the disk; and the one path that runs arguments read back from disk (the recovery re-issuing an interrupted create) runs only what `_replayable` kept whole. `test_elision.py` pins the first two, watching the disk while the tool runs, and `test_resume.py` pins the third. The evidence that settled it was the geometry: an argument excerpt's tail is never longer than `_ARG_ELISION_TAIL`, and the posted one was longer. **Before blaming a cap for a marker, check whether the cap could have drawn that shape.** So `Engine._run_tool` refuses any call whose arguments carry one of the harness's markers and tells the model why (`_elision`). This is the Mining Boundary's rule, *the harness never files its own words as the agent's*, read in the other direction. Three properties a later edit will break:

- **One template per marker.** The markers are rendered, recognized, and (for `_context`'s cut-short opening) derived from the templates in `_elision`, so rewording one cannot leave the guard matching nothing. A wording the harness stops writing stays in `_LEGACY` for as long as a transcript can hold it, because the cap's fixed point re-saves an old excerpt byte for byte on every wake.
- **The refusal says what happened on *its* seam.** A refused live call was never sent. A create the recovery is re-issuing was already attempted by a dead wake, so its outcome is unknown. Telling the model "nothing was sent" there invites a rewrite under the next ordinal, which is a second post.
- **Only markers that stand in for content the reader no longer has belong in the set.** Harness text that is true when quoted (`shell`'s truncation note, `INTERRUPTED`, a step note) is not an excerpt of anything, and refusing it would refuse legitimate forwarding. A **new** stand-in marker needs a template in `_elision`, or it is text the agent can hand back as its own.

**The unit of a cap is the *step*, never the call — and the reason is the whole of #304.** `max_steps` bounds the model's *calls*, not the tools it dispatched: a model may emit several tool calls in one assistant turn (every model the fleet runs does), so a per-*call* cap let a step's persisted growth scale with a fan-out nothing bounds, and the inequality below — counting one call per step — understated the worst case by exactly that factor, silently and without limit. A step's results now share one `TOOL_RESULT_CAP` and its calls' arguments share one `TOOL_ARGS_CAP`, **water-filled** (`_session._fill`), which is what makes the fix free: a lone call gets the whole budget and is byte-for-byte what it always was, and a wide fan-out of *small* results keeps every one of them whole (the smallest of any set is never bigger than its mean, and its share **is** that mean) — only a fan-out that is also **fat** pays. That same property is what makes the cap a **fixed point**: an already-capped set fits, so re-saving it every turn for the life of the timeline never grinds an excerpt into an excerpt of an excerpt. The one thing that does *not* fit its share, the stub a wide enough fan-out leaves (`_arguments_stub`, bigger than a ~40-character share), is recognized by its shape and left alone (`_session._is_stub`); before that, the next save re-stubbed it under the size *of the stub*, and every save after kept the wrong number.

**Capping the *dispatch* was the tempting fix and it is the wrong one — worth knowing, because it will look right again.** Refusing to *run* a call does not un-write the call: the assistant turn records every call the model **emitted**, and it must, because dropping one changes what `_idempotency.creates` counts and a drifted ordinal is a message posted twice. A dispatch cap therefore leaves the persisted growth exactly as unbounded as it found it, while refusing legitimate model behavior. The bound has to fall where the bytes land, not where the work is issued.

**A shared budget can reach content a per-call budget never could — and the first thing it reached was the recovery's own sentinel** (caught reviewing #304, never shipped). `INTERRUPTED` is matched **exactly**: `_replayable` keeps an interrupted create's arguments whole because of it, and `_idempotency.interrupted` finds the calls to re-issue by it. At ~342 characters it was never at risk against a 4 KB *per-call* cap — but share that cap across a step and a fan-out of ~14 drives every share below it, at which point the marker is elided to `[... 342 chars elided ...]`, `_replayable` flips to `False`, **the create's arguments are capped and the recovery re-posts the peer's message with its body cut out**, and the call is no longer even recognizable as one to re-issue. So an interrupted result sits **outside the pool** — never charged, never elided — the mirror of the exception `_replayable` already makes on the arguments side. The rule generalizes past this one marker: **the recovery's evidence is never bounded away**, and any new cap must be checked against every sentinel a later wake reads out of the transcript by value.

**What the caps bound, stated precisely, because the residue is not a leak.** They govern the payloads the harness persists *on the model's behalf* — the one class of content that can be arbitrarily large **independently of what the model wrote** (a three-token call can return a 200 KB mailbox). What is left in a step is the model's *own* output: its assistant text, one `id`+`name` envelope per call, and — past ~50 parallel calls, where a share no longer buys an excerpt — one short `gone` marker per call (`_elision.gone`) saying how much is missing. None of that can be dropped (a missing result dangles its call **permanently**; missing arguments blind `create_kind`), and none of it needs to be: it is one small record per call *the model chose to make*, and the provider bounds it at every response's max-output-tokens. So the invariant to reason from is **a step's growth is bounded by what the model wrote, never by what its tools returned** — multiply the tools' output fiftyfold and the transcript does not move (`test_session.py`).

**And every one of those bounds is now *auditable from outside the box*** (issue #369, driven by basecradle-noc#388). The three invariants above say what may grow and what bounds it; nothing said which of them was actually carrying the weight on a given agent, so a standing @glm-5.2 at ~494 K input tokens per call could not be ruled intended-or-defect at all. `Session._drive` therefore emits one `context attribution` line per assembled turn — tool schemas, the brief broken down by *its own composition parts* (charter, `initialize.md`, tool manifest, dashboard, recalled memory), the transcript by role plus the three things a `system` turn can be (charter, compaction summary, step ledger), and image payload — measured off the list about to be handed to `Engine.run`, never re-derived from a config (the `overlay_tool_stems` principle applied to context). Three properties are the design and each is breakable by a well-meaning edit: the sections **sum exactly** to `total`, or a share computed off it describes a payload nobody assembled; the unit is **characters as the model reads them** (`_context.message_chars`, `ensure_ascii=False`) and it is *named on the line*, because a token count would need a per-model tokenizer the harness cannot honestly get — the `llm` line that follows carries the provider's own `tokens_in` for that very payload, so the pair **is** the calibration, measured rather than assumed; and it reports **measurements and no verdict**, exactly as 0.89.0/0.90.0 did. A new *kind* of content added to the message list needs a section here as well as a bound (see above) — folded silently into an existing one, it is a cost nobody can see even though the total moved.

**Written as an inequality, that first guard has a precondition — and it is operator-breakable from both sides** (issue #287). The 50% threshold is a safe distance only while

> `limit × (1 − COMPACT_AT)` **>** `persisted_step_cap() × max_steps ÷ WORST_CASE_CHARS_PER_TOKEN`  (where `persisted_step_cap() = TOOL_RESULT_CAP + TOOL_ARGS_CAP`)

— the headroom above the threshold must exceed what one tool-heavy turn can add (at the shipped constants: 6,144 × 24 ÷ 3.0 ≈ **49,152 tokens**, so the guarantee needs a ceiling of ~**98,304** and the 128 K floor clears it *by construction*). Both caps are in that sum, and the argument half is why (#301): counting only the result was not a tight estimate but a **wrong** one, because the term it omitted was *unbounded* — the inequality did not hold at all. **Two env knobs move those terms in opposite directions:** `HARNESS_MAX_CONTEXT_TOKENS` shrinks the left side, `HARNESS_MAX_STEPS` grows the right. Either can walk an agent out of the guarantee, and the failure is invisible — a turn under a too-small budget crosses from under-threshold to over-ceiling in one step, compaction (which runs only *between* turns) never gets a chance, and the agent silently falls back on the over-length rescue (`emergency_compact` + retry) with nobody told it left the primary mechanism. We did exactly this to @pinky ourselves (`HARNESS_MAX_CONTEXT_TOKENS=20000`, basecradle-noc#218) and the harness said nothing. So `ContextBudget` now **warns at budget resolution, derived from the constants and never a hardcoded number** — and **warns, never refuses**: the override is the 2 a.m. escape hatch and must always win (the same reason `0` is honored as "compaction off"). *The defect was the silence, not the setting.* The warning is keyed on the **arithmetic, not the source**, but its **remedy is keyed on the source**: an `env` ceiling is the operator's number, so raising it is offered; an `adapter`/floor ceiling is the *model's real window*, so raising `HARNESS_MAX_CONTEXT_TOKENS` past it would push the threshold **beyond the wall** — strictly worse than the warning — and only a lower step budget is offered there.
